# Hyperliquid reference

goodcryptoX talks to Hyperliquid directly from your own computer or server. This page says what it reads, how it keeps up, and what it refuses, so you know what the numbers in the panel mean. To connect an account, see [Connect a Hyperliquid account](../venues/hyperliquid.md).

## What goodcryptoX reads

- **Prices and the orderbook**, live: mid prices of every market (about every 5 seconds), the orderbook at the grouping you choose, public trades and one-minute candles.
- **Your account**, live: your orders as they change, your fills, funding payments, liquidations, orders the exchange cancelled on its own, your leverage and what you can still open per market.
- **Your balances**, per collateral coin (USDC, and the other coins some HIP-3 markets settle in), in three figures: *Total*, *Withdrawable* and *In use*. What they include depends on your account mode (standard, unified or portfolio margin), which goodcryptoX reads from the exchange every minute instead of assuming it. In unified and portfolio-margin accounts the exchange says the per-market-group figures are not meaningful, so goodcryptoX does not add them up.
- **Every market**, including HIP-3 markets (names like `xyz:TSLA`), with their exact price and size rules, refreshed every hour.

The live streams, every account's and all prices, come over one WebSocket connection; the exchange's REST interface is the backup and the double-check. Hyperliquid's limits are per internet address, not per connection, so a second connection would add nothing. Every connected account's live updates stay open while goodcryptoX runs, with the panel closed and no bot or order of goodcryptoX on it too, so a fill of an order you placed elsewhere is always seen. Prices, the orderbook, public trades and candles stream only while a screen, a resting order or a bot needs them.

## How goodcryptoX keeps up

- **Losing the connection.** goodcryptoX reconnects by itself, waiting 1, 2, 4… up to 30 seconds between attempts and never more than 30 new connections a minute, and subscribes again to everything it had. After every reconnect it re-reads your orders, fills and balances over REST, so nothing that happened while it was away is missed. If Hyperliquid has not taken the connection again for a minute, a person is alerted once.
- **Hyperliquid's limits per internet address.** Measured on 3 October 2026: 15 accounts with live updates (Hyperliquid's documents say 10), 1,000 subscriptions, 2,000 messages a minute (documented). goodcryptoX does not trust these numbers: it follows Hyperliquid's answers. It sends at most 2,000 messages a minute, holding any more for a moment. When Hyperliquid refuses a subscription because its subscriptions are full, a price stream that only a screen shows (an orderbook or a chart you look at) makes room for a minute, and that block shows its data as unavailable; a bot's or an order's prices are never the ones to make room.
- **Knowing a feed is alive.** goodcryptoX tracks, for every subscription, the exchange's own time on the last message and when it arrived. A market that is merely quiet is told apart from a feed that has stopped by the channels the exchange sends on a timer, never by trades alone.
- **Two orderbooks of the same market.** The exchange does not say in a book message which grouping it was sent for, so the orderbook at a coarser grouping, while the finest one of the same market is also shown, comes on a second connection: each message is attributed by the connection it came on. This is the one case of a second connection.
- **Fills in the same millisecond.** Several fills often share one millisecond. goodcryptoX puts them in the order the exchange executed them, following each fill's starting position; if that order cannot be told, it says so and re-reads your position rather than guessing.

## What goodcryptoX refuses

- **A price or a balance sent as a bare number.** Every amount must arrive as text so that no digit is lost. If the exchange ever sends one as a number, goodcryptoX drops that message, keeps the last good value with its time, and tells you once.
- **An order that breaks the exchange's rules.** Prices have at most 5 significant figures and at most `6 − size decimals` decimal places (whole numbers are always allowed); sizes are cut down to the market's size step, never rounded up; an order worth less than 10 USDC at its own price or at the mark price is not sent, except closing a whole position with a reduce-only order.
- **More requests than the exchange allows.** goodcryptoX spends at most 800 of the exchange's 1,200 request weight per minute, so a Hyperliquid tab in your browser on the same connection still works, and it always keeps room for protective orders. Unified and portfolio-margin accounts may send 50,000 actions a day; when goodcryptoX cannot read your mode it assumes the limit, and near it only protective actions go out.
- **Account changes.** The key goodcryptoX holds may place and cancel orders and set leverage and isolated margin; it changes an order by cancelling it and placing a new one. It is never used to change your account mode.

## Maintenance

goodcryptoX reads Hyperliquid's own status (`exchangeStatus`) when it starts and when every live feed goes silent at once. When the status names a maintenance window, goodcryptoX sends nothing to Hyperliquid until a later reading names none: a new order or bot that would open or add to a position is refused with "Hyperliquid is in maintenance: new orders are refused until it ends; exits and cancels wait and go when it ends." (the order form shows it on its button as the refusal it is, not as "Unconfirmed"); an order of goodcryptoX's that would add risk (a stop entry that fires, a bot's entry) waits; a Close, a cancel of an order of goodcryptoX's, a bot's Stop and a stop-loss or take-profit that fires are taken and wait too. An answer from Hyperliquid itself cannot wait, so adding or removing isolated margin, changing leverage and cancelling an order placed outside goodcryptoX are refused with "Hyperliquid is in maintenance: margin, leverage and cancels of orders placed outside goodcryptoX are refused until it ends; try again then."; press them again after the window. Orders resting on Hyperliquid are never cancelled for maintenance. Sixty seconds after the first reading goodcryptoX checks that it is real: Hyperliquid's mids still stand (it does not trade). Then the Dashboard's "Needs your attention" shows **Hyperliquid maintenance** with since when, and, if you have an open order or a bot on Hyperliquid, Telegram tells you it began. If the status says maintenance but prices move and reads answer, the line says "something went wrong" and what waited goes at once. During the window goodcryptoX reads the status and the mids every 30 seconds, and the feeds' delay alerts wait. A window has no time limit: the line keeps saying since when. When the status names none again, goodcryptoX checks 5 seconds later, then every 10 seconds, that it really ended: the status is clear, the mids answer and move, and your resting orders are still on Hyperliquid's list (one that is missing is looked up by its id, and the line says so meanwhile). Only then everything that waited goes by itself, in the order it came, however long it waited; if the status clears within the first minute, the same check runs at once. The line, and Telegram if it said the start, say it ended at the first order Hyperliquid accepts again, or at the check when nothing waited. Hyperliquid answering with errors and its live connection going quiet without its status naming maintenance is not a window: orders are sent, and one with no answer is looked up by its id, as at any time.

**The minutes after an upgrade.** For 2 to 3 minutes after Hyperliquid's weekly upgrade it refuses every market and limit order with "Only post-only orders allowed immediately after network upgrade", and takes cancels and post-only orders. goodcryptoX follows that rule: post-only orders and cancels go at once; every other order waits in the order it came, and the oldest is sent again every 10 seconds under the same client order id until Hyperliquid takes one (no read shows when the minutes end; an accepted order does). No page, no repair. The order's row says "Waiting: post-only after the exchange's upgrade", and the Dashboard line says "Ending" while the window lasts. An order that left just before the window and got no clear answer is looked up once the window ends: found, it is kept as it is; proven never to have reached Hyperliquid, it is sent again by itself, within about a minute of the end. Until then it shows in repair as unknown, and you get one message about it two minutes after it was sent. The Dashboard and the Trade page say "Hyperliquid maintenance since 08:02" while it lasts. A clear refusal from Hyperliquid is read as what it says; a failure with no clear answer during the window counts as maintenance, not as an unknown error. Hyperliquid's public status page is not read.

## Hyperliquid's own stop-losses

Not offered in this version: stop-losses are goodcryptoX's own. The Settings choice is hidden.

## Every answer has a meaning

Every answer the exchange can give is sorted into one of fourteen kinds, each with one reaction: refused for good; nothing to match, and price moved (the send is not tried again by itself: a stop or exit goes to repair and Retry sends it when you choose); already closed elsewhere; not found (look the order up, fills first); key or approval problem; request-counter problem; too many requests from your connection; your account's request budget used up; exchange maintenance; refused in the post-only minutes after an upgrade (sent again every 10 seconds until taken); a read that got no answer (the figure shows "unavailable", and a person is alerted only after two minutes without any answer); an order or cancel that got no answer (looked up by its own id, never assumed "not placed"); and unknown. An unknown message never cancels anything: it holds new orders and alerts a person. The same goes for order statuses: every status the exchange documents has one meaning, a margin or liquidation cancel puts the bot on that market into repair, and a cancel by the "dead man's switch", which goodcryptoX never arms, alerts a person.

## Connecting your main account

goodcryptoX trades through an **API wallet**: a key that goodcryptoX makes on your computer and keeps in its encrypted key store, which Hyperliquid lets trade your account but never withdraw. Your main wallet approves it; the key itself never leaves goodcryptoX, and your main wallet's key never reaches goodcryptoX.

1. **Connect the wallet that owns your Hyperliquid account** on the connect screen (no signature). goodcryptoX reads from Hyperliquid what it needs: that this wallet is a main account (a sub-account's, a vault's or an API wallet's address is refused with "Connect the wallet that owns the Hyperliquid account"; a wallet Hyperliquid does not know, with "Wallet not linked to Hyperliquid, or the account has < 5 USDC"), your API wallets, whether a referrer is set, and your builder-fee approval.
2. **goodcryptoX makes the API wallet.** Its name is the first of `gcx`, `gcx1`, `gcx2` that your account does not already have, so it never takes the name of another tool's API wallet (an old goodcryptoX 2.0 one is left alone). Hyperliquid allows 3 named API wallets per main account, plus 2 for each sub-account; when they are all taken, the screen lists them and you pick the one to replace (its key stops working); goodcryptoX never offers one whose key it holds itself, and nothing is signed until you pick. The key is written to disk, encrypted, before the screen shows you the address. On a Mac the key store's secret is kept in your login keychain (the item `gcx-ai`); the first account creates the key store.
3. **Your wallet signs "Approve API wallet"** for that address, valid for 180 days less an hour, counted by Hyperliquid's clock, not your computer's.
4. **Only if your builder-fee approval is below 0.03 %, your wallet signs "Approve builder fee" at exactly 0.03 %.** An approval you gave goodcryptoX 2.0 (1 %) stands: no second prompt. goodcryptoX charges 0.03 % whatever the approval allows.
5. goodcryptoX relays each signed message to Hyperliquid as you signed it, field for field, and only these two: the API wallet approval for the address goodcryptoX made for this account, and the builder fee for goodcryptoX's builder at 0.03 %. Anything else your wallet might sign is refused and never sent.
6. **The account becomes active when Hyperliquid lists the API wallet.** From then on goodcryptoX trades it, without a restart; Settings shows the expiry Hyperliquid reports. If you rejected a prompt, the account waits for approval: **Try again** asks for the same signature with the same key.
7. **Referral line:** shown only when your account has no referrer yet. Left checked, goodcryptoX's API wallet sends Hyperliquid's referral code GOODCRYPTOX once, after the account is active (the ticked line "Pay 4% less on Hyperliquid fees", with the grey line "If eligible, join as our referral: 4% off Hyperliquid's fees on trades you place outside goodcryptoX, for your first $25M; not for vaults or sub-accounts"); once Hyperliquid has answered it is never sent again, also not when you replace the API wallet later. If Hyperliquid gave no answer, goodcryptoX tries again at its next start.

You can connect several main accounts (**Add Hyperliquid account**), each with its own API wallet, approved by its own wallet; each main account's sub-accounts and the vaults it leads sit under it, in the account selector and in Settings. They are named Hyperliquid, Hyperliquid-2 and so on, as in goodcryptoX 2.0, and you can rename them. A main account you connected but never approved (the wrong wallet, or a prompt you rejected) stays in the list, waiting for approval, until you press **Try again** or remove it. Removing one main account leaves the others trading. Each API wallet expires on its own date; Telegram reminds you of each, naming its account.

## Sub-accounts and vaults

Your sub-accounts and the vaults you lead trade in goodcryptoX as your main account does: orders, Close, take profit and stop loss, and bots.

- **Adding one.** After your main account's wallet signs in (the first connect, or **Add Hyperliquid account** later), goodcryptoX lists your main account, its sub-accounts and the vaults it leads, as Hyperliquid reports them, each with a check mark "Add as an account"; the ones already in goodcryptoX are ticked and greyed. **Add** puts each ticked one in goodcryptoX at once, named as Hyperliquid names it; nothing more is signed with your wallet, and nothing is added without your tick ([Connecting your Hyperliquid account](../panel/connect.md#the-account-list)). Create sub-accounts and vaults, and move funds to them, in Hyperliquid's app.
- **Who signs.** Sub-accounts and vaults have no keys of their own: your main account's API wallet signs their orders, naming the sub-account or the vault (Hyperliquid's `vaultAddress`). One approval and one renewal every six months cover them all. Hyperliquid removes a main account's API wallets when the main account holds no funds, and then every sub-account and vault stops trading too: keep some funds on the main account.
- **The fee.** Their orders carry goodcryptoX's 0.03 % as your main account's do; only your main account's wallet can sign the approval. That Hyperliquid takes the fee on a sub-account's or a vault's orders is not proven yet: if it refuses an order there for the fee, that order ends with Hyperliquid's words and goodcryptoX never sends it without the fee; each later order tries again, and Hyperliquid answers each one.
- **Balances.** Each account has its own balances, positions and orders; the Dashboard shows each. A vault you lead counts **your own share** of it (what Hyperliquid reports as your equity in the vault), never your followers' money; the vault's whole figures are what you trade it with.
- **How many.** goodcryptoX trades up to 100 Hyperliquid accounts at once, the main account included. Hyperliquid streams live updates for a limited number of accounts per internet address (15 when measured; Hyperliquid's own app open in your browser on the same connection counts too), and goodcryptoX takes as many as Hyperliquid accepts. The others are **read every minute** instead (longer when many are: one more minute for about every 13 accounts read this way). An account read this way hears of its fills and of orders cancelled elsewhere up to that late, which can also delay what goodcryptoX does next on it, and funding paid to it is not booked to its bots while it is read this way. Its fills of orders placed outside goodcryptoX are read only while an order of goodcryptoX on it is unsettled, and told on Telegram only when read within three minutes of the fill. Which accounts get the live updates: first those with a running bot, a position goodcryptoX manages or an order of goodcryptoX not settled yet; then the one whose trade form is open; then the most recently used. An account that needs them takes the place of one lower down; one that was refused tries again every 20 seconds. Adding a sub-account or a vault while goodcryptoX already has as many live accounts as Hyperliquid accepts says so: "Hyperliquid allows a limited number of live accounts per connection point; this one updates every minute over the slower path, which can also delay its orders." More than about 15 live accounts would need a second internet connection; that is not built.
- **Orders placed outside goodcryptoX.** With one account live, an order you place in Hyperliquid's app appears in goodcryptoX at once. With several accounts live, Hyperliquid's order messages do not say which account they are for: goodcryptoX tells its own orders apart by their id, and an order placed in Hyperliquid's app on one of them appears at that account's next read (when the lists open, after a reconnect, or at its next minute's read).
- Two of your accounts can trade against each other; Hyperliquid prevents that only within one address.

## Fees

Every order goodcryptoX sends carries its 0.03 % in the exchange's own builder-fee field, at goodcryptoX's builder address; stop-losses, take-profits and closes too, so nothing is ever sent without it. goodcryptoX does not check your approval before an order leaves: each order tries, and Hyperliquid answers. If you revoke the approval while orders or bots run, Hyperliquid refuses their next order; goodcryptoX tells you once where to approve again and does not send it without the fee: a plain order ends refused, and an order that was already working, such as a stop-loss that fired, waits in repair with Hyperliquid's reason. goodcryptoX adds no other identifier to your orders.
