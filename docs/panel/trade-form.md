# Trade screen: the order form, the selectors and the layout

On the right of the trade screen sits the order form. It places manual orders on the instrument on screen, and it is where the bots are started. Above it you choose the account and the instrument; the rest of the screen is laid out in columns you can resize and rearrange.

Choose pairs, sort 24-hour volume/change with the [Instrument selector](instrument-selector.md).

## Orders or Bots

The switch at the top of the form chooses between **Orders** (the order form; goodcryptoX's word for it, `orderFormGroup.manual`) and **Bots** (the bot type and its settings: DCA in the first version). Switching does not clear anything on either side. The form is ready as soon as the page opens: a figure of your account that is still on its way (Available, the leverage, Margin, the value of a share of your balance) shows a small ring in its place, never a zero. Until the leverage is read, pressing Buy or Sell shows "Syncing your margin mode & leverage with the exchange..." and sends nothing; press again once it is there. Your available funds show at the right of the switch, as goodcryptoX 2.0 drew them: the word "Available" small and grey, the figure white and bold, the currency bold beside it in grey. It is the figure of the side you have chosen (see below), and it ends where the form below it ends. While an order, a position or a bot is open, the switch has a third tab for its details (see [Trade screen](trade-screen.md)).

## The rows of the form

The form is goodcryptoX 2.0's, row by row, label at the left. Compact choices keep their text inside fixed rounded corners; every choice row (Order type, Margin, the Position shortcuts, the Stop Loss and Take Profit types, and the same in the DCA form) is a pill strip, a thin glass track with the selected choice in a bubble inside it; Stop Loss and Take Profit use short rows with their switches at the left of the control area. Fields fill the control area. The send button is at the left of the column, sized to its label (at least 200 px), with a little more air above and below it than between the other rows, as goodcryptoX's form had it. The position text is centered in its pill. On a perpetual it starts with the **Position** row: only the current position size in a neutral grey badge, or 0 when no position is open. Entering an order size does not replace that actual figure with a forecast. Under it is the **Buy | Sell** toggle (see below). Then:

- **Order type**: Market, Limit and a third cell that shows the type you last chose from the caret's menu, Trailing at first, selected when it is the current type. Clicking the selected type again does nothing; only the caret opens the menu. The menu always lists Stop Market, Stop Limit, Trailing Stop, Trailing Stop Limit, Reverse Trailing Stop and Reverse Trailing Stop Limit, in that order, each with its explanation, for example "Stop Market: Send Market order once trigger price is reached"; the selected one has a check mark in its place and never moves. No name says Buy or Sell, because the side selector does; the reverse ones keep their nickname by side, (Buy the Dip) or (Sell the Pump). A stop or trailing choice on the strip shows the same explanation when you point at it. All order types use text, without the goodcryptoX mark.
- **Margin** (perpetuals): the margin mode button (Cross or Isolated, each with its explanation; a market that allows only Isolated shows Cross as "Not supported"), the leverage presets up to 20x, and a caret that opens a box under the Margin row for any leverage up to the market's maximum, with the old warning above 20x.
- **Size**: the position shortcuts (Position 25 %, 50 %, 75 %, 100 %) above the field, a glass strip as wide as the field with its five words spread over it, never cut, with no tooltip (with no position they are all disabled and say nothing), based on the absolute open position on either side and showing the computed amount in the field, the field with its unit (base or quote) and − / + buttons (held down, they repeat; typed values move by their last displayed digit; values filled by the app move by a tenth rounded down to one significant figure, at least one order minimum and the venue grid; a size in the quote currency is sent rounded down to the size step, except where that would take it under the exchange's minimum order value while what you typed meets it: then one step up, as the DCA bot does, so 10 USDC at 3 sends 3.34, not 3.33), the slider with Max under it, then Post only (limit orders) and Reduce only (perpetuals), each a term with a dotted underline (hover it, or tap it, for the explanation). Delayed and, on stop orders, Trigger timeout are ticks that open their rows. Time in force is always enabled. Delayed opens its duration (as goodcryptoX 2.0's) and, under it, **Send at**: a date and time, in your browser's time, at which goodcryptoX sends the order instead; Time in force shows its dropdown at the right of its label, with the Settings default of one year. The finite menu is 15m, 1h, 8h, 1d, 1w, 30d, 3m, 6m, 1y and Custom. Picking a duration clears the moment beside it and the other way round. The sentence under the buttons says "Sends at Oct 4, 09:30." or "Cancels at …". A limit price further from the market than the exchange accepts (Hyperliquid's perpetuals: 80 % from their price) shows a red line beneath Price and the button sends nothing: "Limit price is too far from the market price: the exchange will not accept it". A moment that is not ahead of now, or a Cancel at not after the send, is refused by goodcryptoX; its reason is named beneath the button, and a refusal naming Send at or Cancel at also appears beside that date field.
- The order type's own fields: Trigger price; Trailing distance (% or the quote currency) with Improve only under it (not on a reverse trailing stop); Trailing start price, with a small grey **Optional** beside its label and the field left empty; Price, each with − / + by the last displayed digit when typed, or the field’s tick when filled by the app. **Expected price** shows the book estimate for Market orders; Stop Market and Stop Limit do not repeat the trigger as an Expected price row. A trailing order keeps its own distance and optional start fields.
- Below the exits and above the order button, the same block the bot form ends with: **Order value** and **Margin** side by side, each a dotted label, the figure and a line under it (the order's size in the coin under Order value; what is available under Margin, with a bar of how much of it the order locks). The block has its own row, with space above it and before Send. On spot there is no margin: Order value stands alone, in the first place. A figure with no size yet reads "–". goodcryptoX had one line for the two.
- **Stop Loss** and **Take Profit** as switches that open their sections as before (below).
- One left-aligned 50 px button, taller than the 40 px fields and with air above and below, in the colour of the side you chose, whose words say what the order does to your position (below), and under it the old sentence, for example "Buy 0.5 contracts of HYPE.USDC at 87.88. If this order is fully filled:" (it starts with the order; the account is named in the selector at the top of the page) followed by one line per stop-loss and take-profit.

The send button, Confirm and Launch are solid in the side's colour, in the house's light type; the send button's own label is bolder.

## One form, the side you choose

At the top of the form a **Buy | Sell** toggle (on perpetuals **Buy / Long** and **Sell / Short**) chooses the side the form works for. The side you chose has its text in the same green or red as the order button, on a slightly lighter segment; the other side is grey. A click, a tap, an arrow key (it moves to the other side) or Tab and Space chooses; hovering never does. It is Buy the first time, then the side you chose last (kept in this browser).

The side you chose decides everything the form says and does:

- the **button** is green on Buy and red on Sell and sends that side's order. Feedback belongs to its field: one small line right beneath Size (under its slider), Price, Stop Loss or Take Profit, closer to its field than to the row below, with a red outline for an error. A problem without a field appears beneath the button. There is no global list or count badge; field feedback stays quiet until you touch that field or try to send;
- its words are what the order does to your position: on spot **Buy** or **Sell**; on a perpetual with no position **Open long** or **Open short**; with a long, Buy is **Add to long** and Sell is **Reduce long**, becoming **Close long** when the size is exactly the position (or Reduce only, which stops at zero) and **Reverse to short** when it is more; with a short the same the other way (**Add to short**, **Reduce short**, **Close short**, **Reverse to long**); until you type a size Sell on a long reads Reduce long and Buy on a short Reduce short; while your position has not been read yet it reads **Buy / Long** or **Sell / Short**, since a position not read is not called flat;
- after a press, the order's message at the bottom right says that it was placed or executed (or why not), once the exchange answered, with **Details** to open it (see [Trade screen](trade-screen.md), "Messages at the bottom right"); the line under the button keeps what you may need to act on, starting with the side's name: the exchange's refusal, or "Unconfirmed, check open orders";
- the chart draws that side's take-profit and stop-loss lines (a long's take-profit above the entry, a short's below), and the text under the button describes that side's order;
- **Available** at the top of the column is that side's: on a perpetual the margin the exchange makes available for a long or for a short (they differ once you hold a position); on spot your quote balance for a buy and your base balance for a sell;
- the Position row's arrow is that side's: a Buy of 0.005 on a long of 0.0098 reads "0.0098 → 0.0148" (no word before it, and nothing at all until a size is typed), a Sell "→ 0.0048". An order that goes Reduce only (the box, or the exact close below) stops at zero in that arrow, never past it: a Sell of 0.02 on that long reads "→ 0", while the same Sell without Reduce only reads "→ -0.0102", a short. While your position has not been read yet the chip is a grey shape, never a zero. Spot has no Position row yet.

## The confirmation

A clean order asks one short question: the order's whole description, the same words the form shows under the button (the order, then what happens if it is fully filled: the stop loss and the take profits). **Confirm** is on the left in the order’s side colour; **Back** on the right sends nothing. **Do not ask again** sits below the buttons and is kept in this browser; **Settings › General › Trading › Confirmation dialogs › Placing an order** turns it back on. The receipt and position/value figures remain in the form. Back, the ×, Escape or a press outside closes it without sending.

Problems appear at their fields as compact lines, or under the button when no field owns them. The Size field follows goodcryptoX: once you type in it, a size it cannot take rings it in red at once, and its line comes with your press; a size set by a shortcut, the slider or Max says its line at once; switching the unit starts over. Empty/unreadable required input and no chosen account stop the order: the button stays a button, and pressing it shows what is missing where it is missing (an empty Size gets a red ring and its line), as goodcryptoX's form did; nothing is sent. Exchange-informed checks can be wrong, and a check never stops an order the exchange can answer (a limit further from the price than the exchange accepts included, its line red under the price): the first press shows their warning wording and changes the button to **Send anyway**, without a dialog or order request. Press it again to send the current order with only the existing warning overrides you saw. This warning path is its own confirmation even when clean-order confirmation is on; it does not change that preference. The runtime still applies its own checks and may refuse the order, with its answer below the button.

Every form edit resets the warning acknowledgement. A warning appearing, disappearing or changing its field also resets it. An updated receipt or translated wording with the same warnings does not: prices, available funds and Max/percent quantities keep updating, and the second press uses the current command. All warnings being acknowledged appear in the relevant field’s single compact block; there is no global list or count badge. Pressing Enter in a field never sends an order.

Every term with a dotted underline in the form (Margin, Post only, Reduce only, Improve only, Order value, Trigger timeout, Trailing Stop Loss, TP type and the others) opens its explanation where you point. On the words of a checkbox (Post only, Reduce only, Improve only, Trailing Stop Loss) the explanation shows only when you point with a mouse; pressing or tapping them ticks the box, so a phone shows no card there.

While an order is being sent its button stays disabled until your goodcryptoX runtime answers. If the answer is lost or the connection fails, the form sends the same order once more with the same identity (the runtime never places it twice). If there is still no answer, the button stays disabled; when no order has appeared ten seconds after the press, the form says "Unconfirmed, check open orders" and lets you continue. It never tells you an order was not placed when it cannot know: only the runtime's own refusal is shown as one.

## Close

When you have a position, **Close** closes all of it at market, reduce only. It sends no size: your runtime sizes it from the position at the moment it sends it. It asks first; tick "Do not ask again" to skip that. Like the order button, it stays disabled until your runtime answers. To close part of a position, press a position shortcut (25 %, 50 %, 75 %, 100 %), choose Sell (or Buy for a short) and press the button. A shortcut sets the size only, as in goodcryptoX 2.0; tick Reduce only yourself if you want it. When the size you set closes the whole position exactly (100 %, or the size typed), the order on the side that closes it goes Reduce only by itself: its description and the confirmation say "reduce only", Hyperliquid takes the order even under 10 USDC, and no minimum warning shows. The box stays as you left it, and the side that adds is not affected.

When one of your DCA bots holds part of the position, Close says so under the Position row, beside Close ("0.4 BTC of this position is DCA BTC's: this order sells the bot's coins and makes its PnL wrong. Not recommended."), and so does a reduce-only order larger than your own part, among the form's messages. It is a warning only: nothing is refused, and the order goes as you send it.

## What the form remembers

The form keeps what you entered, in this browser, across instruments, venues, the Orders | Bots switch and every order you send. Nothing resets when you change the instrument. What happens to each value depends on what it is:

| Value | When you change the instrument |
|---|---|
| Order type, flags, units, percentages, TP/SL percentages, number of targets and their shares, time in force, delay, trigger timeouts, a size as a percent or Max | Kept as they are. |
| Prices: limit, trigger, trail start, a TP/SL at a fixed price or distance, a trailing distance in the quote currency | Kept for the instrument they were set on and cleared here; when you come back to that instrument, its own prices return. An empty, untouched limit or trigger price starts from the first last price received for this instrument, rounded to its price rule. Typed and restored values win. An optional trailing start stays empty for automatic activation. |
| A size in the base asset (for example 0.5 BTC) | Kept at the value it had, converted to the new instrument at its first price (0.5 BTC worth 50,000 USDC becomes 16 ETH at 3,125). Until that price arrives the size is empty. |
| A size in the quote currency (for example 250 USDC) | Kept when the new instrument has the same quote currency, cleared otherwise. |

These changes show directly in the fields, without a notice bar. If the new venue does not offer the order type or a flag you had, the form uses the venue's default or turns the unsupported flag off. Pending base-size conversion is still saved at the first price of the correct instrument.

Repeat, Reverse and Increase from your lists change the instrument first and then fill in the order's own price and size.

Leverage is never part of the form: it is the exchange's setting for that instrument (below).

## Size and units

Type the size in the base asset or in the quote currency (the unit selector next to the size). On perpetuals the quote amount is the order value, never the margin. Your runtime converts a quote amount to the base size it sends: at your limit price for a limit order, at the trigger for a stop, at the expected first stop for a trailing order, and at the best price on the other side of the book for a market order; the confirmation shows the result.

The 25 %, 50 %, 75 % and Max buttons and the slider size the order as a share of what is available for each side, worked out separately for Buy and Sell. On spot, Max on Buy spends your whole quote balance and Max on Sell sells your whole base balance.

Typed amounts complete to the selected unit’s step when you leave the field: the instrument’s base size step, or one unit of quote. Switching base ↔ quote converts a typed amount at the current order reference price and floors it to the new unit’s step. Without a reference price, the converted amount stays empty. Max and percentage sizing retain their meaning when you change units.

The − / + buttons step the current resolved size and respect the known minimum; a slider drag respects it once sizing is available. A full opposite reduce-only close keeps its existing below-minimum exception. You can still type an amount below the minimum to see its validation feedback; typing does not silently clamp it.

## Leverage and margin

On perpetuals the form shows the exchange's margin mode (Cross or Isolated) and leverage for the instrument, as the exchange reports them. Changing them is sent to the exchange on its own. While the change waits, the Margin control is disabled and a small ring appears beside its label, without moving the form. Once the answer is read, the exchange’s current value is shown, yours or not. A failed read ends the wait and retains the last known value. Where a bot trades the coin, a manual change is sent all the same, as in goodcryptoX 2.0: the bot trades at what the exchange has. An order goes at the leverage the exchange has when it is sent, as in goodcryptoX 2.0 (until 2026-10-05 goodcryptoX refused an order when the exchange's differed from the one you saw).

A change of leverage or margin mode is sent to the exchange once. A leverage above the market's maximum, or a margin mode it does not offer, is refused before anything is sent ("Invalid Leverage"); the exchange's own refusal is shown with goodcryptoX 2.0's sentence ("You have insufficient available balance to use as margin with the selected Position Leverage. Position Leverage restored to previous value."). While the account holds a position on the market, the Margin control draws the other mode grey with "Close the position first" beside it, in the order form, the bot form and a position's details; it can still be picked. A margin mode changed while the account holds a position on that market is refused by Hyperliquid, and the form says to close the position first ("Close the position to change the margin mode"). When the exchange confirms, the form shows its leverage within about a second. If the exchange does not answer, goodcryptoX never sends the change again: once it can no longer arrive, goodcryptoX reads the leverage the exchange has and tells you whether your change is there.

Isolated margin on a position can be added or removed with `POST /api/v1/margin` (in the panel, the Margin row's pencil in a position's details): in the collateral coin, in the exchange's allowed steps, sent once; it is not repeatable safely, so with no answer goodcryptoX says so and never sends it again: check the position's margin on the exchange before trying again. Where a bot trades the coin, both are sent all the same.

## Order types and their fields

What each type and tick does, in a trader's words: [Order types and options](../trading/order-types.md).

As many order types as fit are one tap away, with the selected type kept visible (a type chosen from the list takes the last place on the strip); the caret opens the remaining choices and the selected extra type with their explanations: Stop Market, Stop Limit, Trailing Stop, Trailing Stop Limit, Reverse Trailing Stop (Buy the Dip or Sell the Pump) and Reverse Trailing Stop Limit.

- **Trigger price** and **trigger timeout** (stops): the timeout's row is always there, None by default, like the Stop Loss's SL timeout; the timeout waits that long after the price first crosses and fires only if it is still across.
- **Trailing distance** in percent or in the quote currency, and **trail start** (trailing starts when the price reaches it; empty: from placement). Switching the distance's unit keeps the distance: 1 % from a trail start of 100,000 becomes 1,000 USDC (from the last price when there is no trail start), each direction rounded down, as goodcryptoX did. **Improve only** makes the first stop your start price.
- **Price** (limit and stop limit) and the order's total.
- **Post only** (limit) and **Reduce only** (perpetuals). Reduce only can be ticked with no position open: it applies when the order executes, and the exchange decides then, as in goodcryptoX.
- **Time in force** always shows for resting orders: how long an order may rest before it is cancelled, from fifteen minutes to one year, or a custom future time within one year. The default comes from your settings (one year unless the exchange allows less); the default is explained in the tooltip. An explicitly set duration keeps its line under the order ("Cancelled if still open after 1 hour"). Where the exchange has no such order, your runtime cancels it on time.
- **Delay**: send the order after a wait.

When you leave a price, trigger or explicit trailing-start field, the displayed value completes to the instrument’s price tick, rounding down on both Buy and Sell. A positive value below one tick completes to one tick. The − / + buttons use the last displayed digit for a typed price and the tick at the current price magnitude for a seeded price. No tooltip and no helper line states the tick or the size step; a digit past them is not taken as you type. Your runtime still applies its own validation and send rules.

Entry trailing distance and manual TP/SL fields complete on blur; absolute prices use the tick at their own current value and quote-price distances retain their price grid. Manual TP/SL percentages and target shares complete to 0.01 percentage points; their buttons move by 0.1. An empty optional trailing minimum profit stays automatic. A field you empty shows nothing, as in goodcryptoX; where a field shows grey text before you type (a share of the available size, a date's pattern), it is never the white of a typed value. Total loss is money in quote, so blur does not impose a price tick on it.

## Take profit and stop loss

Switch on **Take Profit**, choose **Market**, **Limit** or **Trailing** in its shared type control, and add targets one at a time (+1 TP / −1 TP), each with its level (a percent, a distance or a price) and its share of the order; the level under each target is worked out for the side you chose ("Sell at 101,000"). **Trailing take profit** replaces the targets with a trailing distance, a share and an optional minimum profit. A trailing exit's row is labelled **Trailing distance** alone: its section's switch already names it. With Trailing the rows are Trailing distance, Min. profit and Size; the size you set stays when you switch between Market, Limit and Trailing. One **Time in force** row, with "TP and SL" under its label, closes the exits: how long every take profit and stop loss attached to the order stays open, counted from the order's full fill. TP/SL **Reduce only** applies to all targets; it comes from Settings › General › Trading when you send, with no row in the form.

Switch on **Take Profit**, choose **Market**, **Limit** or **Trailing** in its shared type control, and add targets one at a time (+1 TP / −1 TP), each with its level and its share of the order. A new target sits one step further (1 %, 2 %, 3 %; in the quote currency 1, 2, 3; in price the price those percentages give from the order's price); while you have not changed a share, the shares split evenly (50/50, 33.33 each) on every +1 TP and −1 TP, and once you have, a new target takes what is left to 100 %. With two or more targets reaching 99 %, a line says when they close less than the size. The unit menu beside each level is goodcryptoX's: **Price**, **Distance, %** and **Distance** in the quote currency, each with its line ("Trigger if BTC price rises by this percentage" on a buy), and the field shows the unit with an arrow the way the price moves to trigger ("↑ %"); the level under each target is worked out for the side you chose ("Sell at 101,000"). **Trailing take profit** replaces the targets with a trailing distance, a share and an optional minimum profit. TP/SL **Reduce only** applies to all targets; it comes from Settings › General › Trading when you send, with no row in the form.

Switch on **Stop Loss**; it takes a price, a distance in percent or in the quote currency, or the loss you accept (**Expected loss**, shown as "USDC loss"), from the same menu with its lines ("Trigger if BTC price falls to this level" on a buy), or a trailing distance. A stop loss can sit beside a Limit take profit on futures (goodcryptoX 2.0 allowed it there) and on spot (goodcryptoX 2.0 allowed none there); nothing is greyed or refused. On spot: the line under **TP type** says the stop loss is sent only after the take profit is cancelled, and the order's description says the stop loss is goodcryptoX's own and cancels the Limit take profit first ([how it works](../trading/tp-sl.md#on-spot-a-stop-loss-beside-a-limit-take-profit)). **SL timeout** offers None, 1 s, 3 s, 5 s, 10 s, 30 s, 60 s and 120 s. **Follow TPs** (shown once both Stop Loss and Take Profit are on, as the Stop Loss section's last row, after **SL timeout** and directly above the Take Profit switch, so it never pushes a row you already see): *Off* (the stop loss does not move), *Move to breakeven* (at the first target) or *Follow TPs* (to the previous target at each one; with one target it is the same as Move to breakeven). Without a take profit nothing is followed and nothing is sent.

Switching a take profit's or a stop loss's unit keeps its trigger where it is, as goodcryptoX did: on a buy at 100,000 for 0.01 BTC, a stop loss of 1 % becomes 99,000 as a price, 1,000 USDC as a distance and 10 USDC as an expected loss, and every target converts alike. A price or a distance is rounded down to the price step, a percent to 0.01, an expected loss down to the price rule's step. An empty field stays empty; without a price to measure from (or a size, for an expected loss) the field empties rather than keep a number in the wrong unit.

The mark in a stop loss's or take profit's field reads the unit first, then what it is: "USDC price" for a price, "% ↓" or "USDC ↓" for a distance, "USDC loss" for the loss you accept. The arrow is the way the price moves to trigger the exit: down for a Buy's stop loss and a Sell's take profit, up for a Buy's take profit and a Sell's stop loss. A trailing order's distance carries the same arrow (up when its trigger sits above the best price). The unit is your pair's quote currency (USDT on a USDT pair), and in a language that puts the word first the order follows the language, the same in every mark.

## Choosing the instrument and the account

With no account connected the form is the same form: a side chosen, Position 0, every field, shortcut, slider and switch working on the instrument's own figures, and the Order value from the size you type. Only the send button differs: it connects an account (**Connect Hyperliquid account** on Hyperliquid) and opens Settings. Available is not shown; the margin and leverage row works on the form alone (it opens on 5x, or the coin's maximum where that is lower, you can pick Cross or Isolated and a leverage, the Margin figure follows, nothing is sent), and the account's own leverage replaces your pick once you connect. A size is never refused for a balance there is no account to read. The same holds for the bot form, whose Launch becomes the same button.

The account and the instrument are chosen in the strip at the top left of the page; see [Trade screen](trade-screen.md). The account list shows one row per venue account with its status; disabled and empty accounts are hidden, except an account whose API wallet is still waiting for approval, which shows **Checking...**.

The instrument list has the tabs **Favorites**, **Spot** (the venue's spot markets, traded with Buy and Sell; see [Spot trading on Hyperliquid](../trading/spot.md)) and **Perps**; empty tabs are hidden. Search matches the pair, the base, the quote and their ids, including a dex prefix such as `xyz:`. The three instruments you opened last come first, then exact and starting matches, each group in name order. The column on the right shows either the 24-hour volume or your balances (base and quote). Tap the star to add a favorite; favorites are kept by your runtime, so every browser sees them.

## The layout

On a wide screen (1,600 px and more) there are three columns: the chart with the instrument bar and your lists under it; the orderbook and the trades; your position, the form and one more tab group. Between 768 and 1,600 px there are two columns: your lists stay under the chart, and Book and Trades are tabs under the form. On a narrower screen everything is in one column: the chart, the form under it (on a phone folded to its tabs and Available until you tap it), then the lists. The gear in the page header can keep two columns from 768 px, or three from 1,010 px (see [Trade screen](trade-screen.md)).

Drag the bars between columns and rows to resize them (or focus a bar and use the arrow keys); double-click a bar to put that row back. Drag a tab onto another group to move it; it keeps its place and its data. The form has no scroll bar of its own: on a window too short for it, the column grows with the form and the page scrolls.

Your layout is remembered in this browser, one for each screen size. The cog next to it asks "Do you want to restore default layout?", then puts the current screen size back to the default.

Machine-readable: [instrument row](../reference/schemas/instrument-row.schema.json), [saved form state](../reference/schemas/form-state.schema.json), [order command](../reference/schemas/order-command.schema.json).

When trading is off for the account, a line above the send button says why, with **Fix** beside it, as goodcryptoX had it: "Allow builder fees to enable trading" (Fix runs the approval here), or your API wallet has expired or is no longer approved (Fix opens Settings). The send button, and Launch in the bot form, keep their words and stay disabled; pressing one makes the line blink. The approval's wallet progress, refusal and retry show under the line. A warning that some of the position belongs to a bot appears once, below Size.

## Choosing a time

A delayed order's **Send** reads as one sentence with its choice: now, in 5 minutes, in 15 minutes, in 1 hour, in 4 hours, or later… ("Send in 5 minutes"); a DCA bot's **Start** has now, in 1 hour, in 4 hours, in 8 hours, in 24 hours, or custom…. **Later…** (the bot's **custom…**) opens a date-time box that follows your language’s date order, with a four-digit year and 24-hour time; its line echoes the moment in words.

**Time in force** uses one finite-expiry element in Settings, the main order and the shared TP/SL field. Custom replaces its dropdown with DD/MM/YYYY HH:MM and a small × restores the dropdown. Its error names the invalid part; the small line gives only the remaining duration, with hours and minutes below a day. One year is the maximum, and expiry is never remembered from the last order. A custom moment cancels the main order or both TP/SL legs at that exact time; a preset TP/SL duration counts from full fill (or attachment to a position). A past or invalid time must be corrected before sending. Exits set to end more than a year after their entry stay red under the field and are sent with **Send anyway**; a date before the entry expires cannot be sent.

Every **Custom…** (or **later…**) choice in the forms works the same way (Time in force, Send, a DCA bot's Start, the trigger timeout and the SL timeout): the chosen word reads in plain text, and the choice gives way to a field in the same place, with the cursor already in it; a timeout's field holds its unit, **sec** or **min**, at its right.
