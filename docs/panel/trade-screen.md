# Trade screen

The Trade page is goodcryptoX 2.0's trade page: the same places, the same controls and the same words, with the look of the old DEX pages (glass panels, pill tabs, larger fields).

Keyboard focus is shown by a thin accent ring. Mouse clicks stay quiet; Enter opens and closes sections.

## The page

- **At the top left**, one glass strip holds the account selector (the exchange's logo and the account's name) and the symbol selector (the coin, `BASE.QUOTE` and its badge, for example `Perp`). The list leaves out what the exchange itself has delisted (Hyperliquid's `isDelisted`), also under a search and in your favourites; a position or order on such a coin still shows its name. The layout button and the reset cog are at the right of the page header.
- **The account list** shows every account with one mark at the right: a check on the account you are in. An account that is ready says nothing; one that needs a look keeps its warning mark and its words, for example **Builder fee not approved**, **Key Error** or **No Permission**. Sub-accounts and vaults sit under their main account, each with its own status, and trade as it does. An account you have just connected, while its API wallet is still waiting for Hyperliquid, is listed with **Checking...** and is the one the page opens on. There is no balance in this list, as before. Another account on the same exchange keeps the instrument on screen.
- **The symbol list** has a pill with the tabs ★ (your favourites), **Spot** (the exchange's spot markets, with their chart, book and trades) and **Perps** (the exchange's perpetuals), the lists' search at the right of the same row when the exchange lists more than ten instruments, and **24h vol.** and **24h change** columns. Click a heading to sort; the sorted heading shows its triangle. The instruments you picked last sit on top of each tab, with or without a sort. See [Instrument selector](instrument-selector.md) for the details. A star adds or removes a favourite. New: the search is ready to type as soon as the list opens; the arrow keys move through the rows, Enter picks, Escape closes. Just after goodcryptoX starts, the list holds Hyperliquid's main perpetuals and its spot pairs; the markets of the other dexes (HIP-3) join a few seconds later, and the chart, orderbook and trades of a main-dex coin do not wait for them. A spot pair that has traded nothing in the last 24 hours (Hyperliquid's own 24-hour volume of the pair is zero) is not listed, in Spot, in a search or in your favourites, since it is not really trading; it stays listed while it is the pair on screen or you have an order, a position, a bot or a balance of its coin. Perpetuals are always listed.
- **Over the chart**: "Last BTC.USDC price" with the price in large type and, on a perpetual, Index, Mark, Funding and the time to the next funding ("Next in"). These figures refresh together at most twice a second, using the newest values. A changed last price briefly flashes green when it rises or red when it falls. The order form still receives every trade as it arrives.
- **Under the chart**: the tabs **Positions**, **Orders** (with its Open/History switch), **Bots**, and **Orderbook** and **Trades** where the layout puts them (with three columns they have their own column in the middle). The lists cover every account and exchange; each filter (this pair only, this account, everything; the type; the side; in-app only) starts on this pair and this account. See [Lists](lists.md).
- **The right column**: the **Orders | Bots** switch with your available funds, and the order form under it (see [Order form](trade-form.md)). With no connected account, both forms keep their usual fields, empty and disabled. **Connect Hyperliquid account** takes the ordinary submit button’s place and opens the existing Hyperliquid connection steps in Settings with one press. The chart and market data stay live, and the lists are empty. The Clean view shows this same settled empty form; it is not a loading state.
- **Details open in place.** A click on an order, a position or a bot opens its details in the right column, where the form was; the switch gets a third tab naming the thing itself ("Order", "Position", or the bot's own name) with a close mark. The row is always one line: when it is full, Available is hidden while the tab is open and returns when it closes; a name that still does not fit fades at its end. One click goes back to the form and back again; the form keeps what you typed and the details keep their place. Close returns to Orders or Bots. The row stays marked in its list, and the chart shows that order's or bot's lines. On a phone they open the same way, in the form's card under the chart, and the page scrolls to them.
- **Nothing waits as "Loading".** Every block is on the page from the first moment. A block whose data has not arrived yet shows nothing inside its frame for the first fifth of a second, so data that comes at once never flashes a placeholder; after that goodcryptoX 2.0's spinner is centred in its body, small for a row and normal for a card. Numbers never appear as a dash or a zero before they are known, and a tab shows no count until its list has arrived. The order form and the lists never wait for one missing figure: the form is ready at once with a small ring on each figure still on its way, and a list shows the rows goodcryptoX already holds and updates after. An empty message ("No active BTC.USDC orders on Hyperliquid", "No recent trades") appears only when the data has arrived and is empty; late data is greyed with a note. The price header over the chart shows the pair and the last price as soon as they are known, with a small ring on each figure still on its way (index, mark, funding); after 30 seconds without them it is greyed with what it has and the rest left blank, until they arrive. The chart's own loading page is never shown. Where you started something (a cancel, a close, the leverage change, an order being sent, an account being checked) goodcryptoX 2.0's green ring shows in place of the control after the same fifth of a second.
- **Messages at the bottom right** (across the page on a phone), as in goodcryptoX 2.0: each says what came of what you did, once it is known; until then the row shows where it stands ("Cancel requested", Sending). An order you send: placed on the exchange or executed at its price, or why not, with **Details** to open it; a bot you launch: running, with the bot's Details. A cancel: "Order canceled", or "Cancel failed, the order is still open". A change: "Order modified", or "Order rejected, the previous order is back" when the exchange refused it and your order is as it was. Move to break even: "Take profit moved to break even", or that it failed. A close: "Close order placed". Cancel all and Close all count the results ("3 orders canceled"; "2 of 3 orders canceled, 1 still open"). A bot's Stop and a copy say they were done. A message goes by itself after 5 seconds (an order's or a bot's after 30) or with its ×; an error stays until you close it. Hovering a message keeps it; Escape closes the newest. At most five show at a time, the newest at the bottom; they stay when you change page.
- **Only what you look at is streamed.** The orderbook and the trades are streamed while their tab is on screen, the market figures over the chart while the page is shown, the best prices for the form while the form is shown. Leaving the page or hiding the browser tab stops them, and with them the prices, your balances, the leverage and the chart's candles; a stream is kept for three seconds after it goes out of sight, so flipping between tabs does not restart it.

The order and DCA controls fill the available width of their form column. Stop Loss and Take Profit switches sit at the left edge of their control area. The list tabs keep a small 20px inset and join their card with rounded top corners. A normal 10px gap separates the form card from the tabs below on a single-column screen. Splitter handles keep their own grab area and spacing.

Reloading the Trade page brings back the details you had open, on a phone too, as long as that bot, order or position is still listed; closing the details or leaving Trade forgets them.

## The orderbook

The book shows red asks and green bids around the spread. In the vertical layout, **Price · Size · Total** shows the price (at the book's left edge), the size resting at that price and the running sum from the best price outward, spread across the book's width. The shaded bars draw that sum, growing from the left of each row (in the side-by-side book, outwards from the middle, as goodcryptoX drew them). The coin/quote ticker button switches both Size and Total, for example **BTC / USDC**. Quote values are price × size, added exactly; aggregated quote values carry “≈” because a bucket edge differs from its individual order prices.

The book fills its pane, with at least ten complete rows per side and more where they fit, as many above the spread as below it: a side with fewer price levels keeps its rows empty, so the spread stays at the book's centre. It has no scroll bars. If its controls or spread wrap, its pane grows to keep those rows whole; the page scrolls when the window is shorter. Held book data appears immediately when you reopen its tab; a rung or stream waiting for its first frame keeps those rows without a grey no-data flash until the first-frame deadline; a cold book shows its loading shape while the first frame arrives. In Trades each size stands on its bar, at the start of the size column. Click the ticker over the sizes in Trades to switch between coin size and quote value; this choice is shared with the book. Prices retain their exact displayed digits; large sizes/totals use the number rule’s k/M shortening. Fractional trailing price zeros stay muted. Clicking either the price, Size or Total inserts the row’s price into the form’s active price field, even on retained stale rows; an unreachable runtime disables it.

### Aggregation

The selector shows absolute price steps with their quote ticker, such as **1 USDC**, **5 USDC**, **100 USDC**. The venue groups prices and serves a limited number of levels; goodcryptoX does not aggregate the book locally. Buy prices round down and sell prices up to the requested bucket, with the sizes summed exactly. The spread uses the venue’s value where supplied, so it does not mistake the distance between coarse bucket edges for the actual spread. In a narrow pane, the spread can wrap without covering the first book row.

Each coin remembers its native aggregation rung in this browser, including across page navigation and reloads. The same rung can have another absolute step after a price crosses a power of ten: the ×5 native request moves in steps of 5 near BTC 84,000 and 50 above 100,000. The stored choice therefore remains a rung, rather than a fixed number. Where several native requests have the same step at the coin’s tick floor, the selector shows that step once; a previously chosen supported native request remains its representative. No extra venue requests are invented.

Changing the step keeps the current rows and block height until the new stream’s first matching frame. The retained rows keep their own aggregation’s digits and quote approximation. A different coin starts with its own data; old-coin frames cannot fill it. Missing data and stale/absent feed notices retain their existing meaning.

## Trades

The trades stream lists the latest public trades, newest first, up to 100. Green is a trade where the buyer took the offer, red where the seller hit the bid. The bar behind a trade is its size as a share of the largest trade in the list. Your own trades are shown in bold. When you open the screen, the recent trades the runtime already holds appear at once. In a bounded desktop pane, only complete rows are painted; growing the pane reveals more retained trades. Narrow page layouts keep the natural list height.

## When data is late or missing

The runtime watches every market data stream and tells the screen its state:

- **Delayed**: the book or the trades are greyed out with "Delayed since" and the time the data stopped. A click on the book still inserts that row's price, so it does the same whether the book is fresh or not; the grey and the time tell you how old it is.
- **No data**: the runtime says there is no data for the stream. Right after you open the page it says so for a moment while it starts; the screen waits with a spinner rather than saying so, and only when nothing has come for 30 seconds (no book, or for the trades no trade at all, even an empty answer counting as nothing) does the view say "No data from Hyperliquid since" the time. If the stream was live and then stops, the last rows stay, greyed, with the same line; a click on one still inserts its price.
- **Quiet**: a market with no trades for a while is normal; the trades stream is not greyed for that.
- **Runtime not reachable**: when the screen loses its connection to your goodcryptoX runtime, both views grey out with "Runtime not reachable", and a click on the book inserts nothing.

## For developers

The frames the screen receives are described by [`book-frame.schema.json`](../reference/schemas/book-frame.schema.json) and [`trades-frame.schema.json`](../reference/schemas/trades-frame.schema.json).


Forms use quieter grey labels and choices. The selected choice and the numbers you type remain prominent; Available is a modest white number with a grey ticker. Lists use smaller, lighter text with the same row spacing, and details values are calmer without changing their meaning or profit/loss colours.

Text must stay readable at every width, in either theme, including small prices and large totals. A narrow block may shorten a number by the number display rule, use an ellipsis for a label, or scroll sideways; text must never overlap or disappear without an ellipsis.

The Trade tab bar keeps matching space above and below on phones and desktop. The desktop resize handle keeps its own grab area above that gap.


A stopped bot’s coin keeps a price stream only while the Bots list, bot details or Dashboard bots rows are on screen. Leaving that view or hiding the browser releases it after the usual three-second grace. The order form reads bot ownership separately without keeping that stream open.

In the two-column layout, Orderbook shows bids on the left and asks on the right, with best prices at the top and cumulative shading growing away from the middle. The spread sits between the controls and ladders. Three-column and phone layouts retain asks above the spread and bids below. Both keep at least ten complete rows on each side. The book grows when its controls or spread wrap, and the page scrolls when the window is shorter. They retain the aggregation and BTC/USDC controls plus price insertion from live or retained stale rows.

Popups and confirmation questions have a solid theme backing so chart candles and list text cannot show through their contents.

Order types that do not fit stay in the description menu; its arrow always keeps its own space. The book displays whole rows, leaving any fraction of a row as empty space.

Explanations from dotted-underlined terms stay above a fullscreen chart, a sheet or a dialog.

List and details actions use quiet outlined pills; Close, Cancel and Stop have red text without fill. The builder-fee Fix opens the account page and starts its existing approval; Cancel on that page ends the remaining wallet steps.

## Layout and list height

By default the width decides the layout: one column below 768 px, two below 1,600 px (a 14-inch MacBook at full screen, 1,512 px, has two), three from 1,600 px. The gear's **Columns** changes that: **Automatic** (the default) follows the width; **Two** or **Three** keeps that many columns at any width from 768 px until you pick Automatic again or press Restore default layout (a phone always has one column). With three columns the chart's column takes 60 %, the book's 15 % and the form's 25 % of the columns by default (the resize bars between them are not counted); the book's column is never narrower than its figures read whole, nor the form's than the form, and on a narrower screen the chart gives up the difference. The gear holds the time-in-force and TP/SL reduce-only defaults (the same as Settings), **Columns** and **Restore default layout**, which also returns Columns to Automatic. With three columns the Trades block, until you resize it, reaches down to where the lists end, so the columns end on one line. A column or a block you resize is kept in this browser as you left it; anything you never resized follows the default. A resize bar with the keyboard focus moves with the arrow keys along its own direction: Left and Right for a column, Up and Down for a block. Making the window narrower or wider moves the blocks; the details you have open, a field you are editing, a half-typed form, the lists' tab and search and the chart's interval stay as they were.

**On a phone** the order form sits under the chart, folded to one line: its **Orders | Bots** tabs, **Available** and an arrow. Tap the line to open the form in place (tapping Orders or Bots opens it on that tab); the line stays where it is when the form opens, and a tap on its empty space or on the arrow folds it again. It stays open or folded until you close the browser tab. Repeat, Reverse or Add shows the form filled in, in the same card. Your lists come under the form; switching their tabs keeps the page where it is.

By default the page fills the window's height: the chart and the orderbook take most of it and end on one line, the lists and Trades start on one line under them and end at the bottom of the window, as the form does; a long list scrolls inside its block and never moves the chart. In the middle column or under the form the lists reserve room for ten complete default rows plus their tabs, header and paging actions, and the page scrolls below the form when needed. The lower lists have a resize bar in both desktop layouts. A form that always shows its full content needs no lower form bar; the chart and split columns retain their bars. Lists start with 20 rows; Show more adds the next 20 and Show all opens the complete result. The preview has enough orders to show both actions.

In the two-column layout, Trades fills the pane. The date appears beside the time
when there is room; each size bar starts at Size and reaches
through the Price lane. Other layouts keep their compact Trades width.

Both market and account selectors dim the page at the same strength. Menus and tooltips use local glass without dimming the page. Position shows your current size in a grey badge, or 0 when no position is open.

Margin changes wait with one small ring beside Margin, without adding a sentence or moving the form. Its menu explains Cross and Isolated; the leverage dialog shows one warning at a time.

List row content stays vertically centred. Actions keep their familiar outlined look and move to the row's hover or swipe tray before the list folds a column; wider lists keep their actions inline.

Order-type menus show a short description under each name. Choice strips use equal cells where the words fit, and share spare space evenly when they need different widths. The Size shortcuts retain their intrinsic width and 3 px gap above the field. Instrument icons use round masks, and the symbol selector keeps the house gap before its list. Hovering a candle shows five columns: date/time, compact Volume, Open/Close, Change, High/Low.

A **Chart** press on an order, position or bot shows its levels and recent price history together. Opening a bot or choosing its cycle does the same. The first visit to Trade in a tab also fits a held position or resting orders, provided you have not moved the scale or made a drawing. Changing resolution, reconnecting or receiving new prices keeps your chosen view. Switching coins keeps your time zoom and resets the price axis for the new coin.

Every pane has its own height and a resize bar under it: the chart, the lists, the orderbook, Trades and the order form. Dragging a bar resizes that pane only, in the direction you drag; what is under it moves with it. Under the chart the lists' bar is free; in the middle column or under the form the lists never go below ten complete rows plus their tabs, header and paging actions. The order form is as tall as its content until you drag its bar; shorter than its content, it scrolls inside itself, so the chart and the send button can share one screen. A double-click on a bar returns that pane to its default height. The sizes are remembered in this browser: once you resize a pane, the heights stay as you left them when the window changes size, and Restore default layout fits them to the window again. The page scrolls when a column is taller than the window, and stays still while you drag.

Lists start with 20 rows; Show more adds the next 20 and Show all opens the complete result. The preview has enough orders to show both actions.

The spread highlights its value; the label, unit and percentage stay quiet. The column resize handles appear when you point at them, anywhere along a column: a thin bar two thirds of the column's height, like the horizontal handles turned vertically.

The position’s head shows its margin mode before its leverage, for example **Isol. 10x** or **Cross 5x**.
